The problem

Scheduling a clinic day is a puzzle that fights back

  • Doctors with different specialties and hours
  • Rooms with special equipment (ECG, ultrasound)
  • Patients who can only come at certain times
  • No double-booked doctors, no double-booked rooms
Placing one appointment is easy. Placing all 30 so that every rule holds is where spreadsheets break.

Why AI · why this technique

It's a Constraint Satisfaction Problem

Formal model

Variables   X_i  = assignment of appointment i
Domains     D_i  = { (doctor, room, slot) } filtered by
                   specialty · equipment · availability
Constraints t_i = t_j  ⇒  doctor_i ≠ doctor_j
            t_i = t_j  ⇒  room_i   ≠ room_j
Goal        complete assignment satisfying all
            constraints — or proof none exists

Why not if-statements?

An if-statement checks one rule for one appointment. We must find a complete combination across 30 appointments × doctors × rooms × slots — combinatorial search.

Why not the other course tools?

  • A* search — no path, all variables assigned simultaneously
  • Bayesian nets — nothing uncertain to infer
  • MDP — no stochastic dynamics or rewards
  • CSP — discrete variables, finite domains, hard constraints ✓
Why AI3:35–4:35
How it works

Backtracking → + MRV → + Forward Checking

1 · Backtracking (BT)

Try a value. If a rule breaks later, go back and try another. Complete but slow.

2 · Minimum Remaining Values

Schedule the appointment with the fewest options first ("hardest patient first"). Fail fast.

3 · Forward Checking

After each assignment, immediately remove now-impossible options from every other appointment. Detect dead ends early.

All three are complete and provably correct; an independent validator re-checks every schedule. Stretch goal: arc consistency (AC-3).

Limitations & what's next

Honest scope today, clear path to V2

POC limits

  • Hard constraints only — no preferences or optimisation
  • One working day, fixed slot length
  • Synthetic (realistic) data, no live clinic yet
  • Exponential worst case — mitigated by MRV/FC and time caps

V2

  • Soft constraints & preferences (weighted CSP / min-conflicts)
  • Multi-day and variable durations
  • Pilot with one real clinic
  • Import from existing booking systems

Backup · Q&A

Questions every team member can answer

  • Why this problem? Real, frequent, combinatorial; no medical risk.
  • Why CSP? Discrete variables, finite domains, hard constraints; scheduling is the canonical CSP.
  • How is it formalised? X_i per appointment; D_i ⊆ doctors × rooms × slots; binary no-clash constraints.
  • How does it work? BT explores; MRV orders variables; FC prunes domains.
  • How do we know it's correct? Independent validator, solver agreement, brute-force on small cases.
  • Limits? Exponential worst case; hard constraints only; synthetic data.
